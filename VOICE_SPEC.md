# BETWEEN THE LINES - Voice Specification

## Voice Requirements (Target Profile)

### Vocal Characteristics
- **Accent:** Neutral American English (NPR/podcast quality)
- **Tone:** Slightly nasal, intellectual, wry
- **Timbre:** Deep, thoughtful, world-weary
- **Age Perception:** Sounds 35-45 years old
- **Emotional Baseline:** Curiosity mixed with quiet frustration (sardonic)

### Delivery Specs
- **Pacing:** 120–140 words per minute (strategic pauses built in)
- **Energy:** Medium-low baseline with spike on punchlines
- **Emotion Arc per Video:**
  1. Calm setup (reading contract)
  2. Slight pause (let it sink in)
  3. Explanation (matter-of-fact)
  4. Punchline (wry laugh or knowing pause)
  5. Call-to-action (matter-of-fact again)

### Signature Vocal Moves
- **Skeptical pause** after revealing absurdity (1-2 second beat)
- **Dry laugh** or breath intake on realization
- **Direct address** to camera (intimate, conversational)
- **No vocal filler** (no "ums," "ahs," or hedging)

### What NOT to Sound Like
- ❌ Angry/shouting (not rant-based)
- ❌ Overly enthusiastic (not hype)
- ❌ Robotic/monotone (needs personality)
- ❌ Condescending (curiosity, not superiority)
- ❌ Generic AI voice (needs character, warmth, imperfection)

## Voice Options to Explore

### Option 1: Professional Voice Actor
**Pros:**
- Natural imperfections and character
- Can deliver specific emotional beats
- Flexible for revisions

**Cons:**
- Higher cost per video
- Turnaround time
- Consistency requires same actor

### Option 2: AI Voice Model (TTS)
**Pros:**
- Instant delivery
- Perfect consistency
- Scalable for 50+ videos

**Cons:**
- Risk of sounding generic
- Limited emotional nuance
- Audience may detect AI

### Option 3: Hybrid (AI Base + Human Voiceover)
**Pros:**
- AI provides base/efficiency
- Human adds character/warmth
- Best of both worlds

**Cons:**
- More complex workflow
- Potentially higher cost

---

## Recommended Direction

**PRIORITY:** Find a voice that sounds like an actual human—someone who sounds slightly tired of explaining obvious things, but is doing it anyway because it matters.

**Core Vibe:** Think NPR journalist who's read too many contracts and can no longer hide their disappointment.
